Venture capital and
strategic technologies

When a strategic industry is hit by cross-border technology restrictions, do investors pull back or move in? And what happens to the innovation that follows?

The questionData and designFindingsInterpretation

The question

Working paper

Export controls are reshaping strategic industries. Most research asks how established firms adjust their supply chains, operations and R&D. We ask what happens to startups, whose survival depends on access to capital.

We study U.S. export controls affecting China's semiconductor industry, with the post-period starting in 2018Q4, and trace their effects on startup financing and subsequent innovation. We also ask what role government venture capital plays when private investors face new uncertainty.

Deterrence or reallocation?

Deterrence. Restrictions raise technological and commercialization risk, so investors delay, screen harder or exit.

Reallocation. Restrictions raise the strategic importance of the sector, so capital is redirected toward technologies seen as nationally important and policy-supported.

Yikai Cao, Wanru Deng, Guankai Zhai and Chuck Eesley · Department of Management Science & Engineering, Stanford University · INFORMS 2026 presentation

Data and design

Data
Zero2IPO Private Equity Database, 2014Q1–2023Q2
Panel
19,485 firms; 560,024 firm–quarters
Treated group
2,783 semiconductor and electronic-equipment firms, from 2018Q4
Comparison group
16,702 firms in other information and communication technologies
  • Difference-in-differences with firm and quarter fixed effects, controls and firm-clustered standard errors; event-study estimates trace the response quarter by quarter.
  • Investor-tie heterogeneity. Triple interactions test whether firms with stronger prior government or private VC ties respond differently.
  • Innovation outcomes. Patent counts and mean external citations over the next quarter and the next four quarters, with prior government VC share (2014Q1–2018Q3) as a moderator.

Findings

The evidence favours reallocation: capital moved toward semiconductor startups after the restrictions, and prior investor ties shaped who supplied it.

Financing shifts toward semiconductor startups

Relative to other ICT firms, the quarterly probability of receiving VC rises by 6.0 percentage points (0.060, SE 0.002). Log financing amounts (+0.108) and stage progression (+0.038) also increase (all p < .01). Event-study estimates grow more positive over time.

More patents, not more cited patents

Patent counts rise over the next quarter (+0.186) and the next four quarters (+0.372, log scale, p < .01). Mean external citations do not improve in parallel (−0.109 over four quarters).

Stronger prior government VC ties moderate this: citations decline less for those firms (+0.124, SE 0.045, p = .006).

Interpretation

  • Individual pre-period estimates vary; the event-study plot alone does not establish parallel trends.
  • Prior government VC ties may reflect investor selection. The moderation result does not identify the causal effect of receiving government VC.
  • The financing and patent analyses use different samples, and the separate results do not establish causal mediation.
  • The semiconductor category includes electronic equipment. Patent counts measure activity; external citations are a proxy for impact and depend on citation maturity.
